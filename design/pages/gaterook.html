Before you connect

A few good questions.

Clear boundaries start with clear answers.

Does it work with my AI?

GateRook speaks MCP, the Model Context Protocol. It includes connection flows for Claude Desktop, Codex, Cursor, and Antigravity. Other clients need to support a compatible MCP transport and authentication method.

Can AI change production on its own?

Changes require human approval, and disabled tools cannot run. You review the exact action and target before approving. You can keep production read-only or expose selected runbooks with approval. The demo shows one possible configuration.

Does any data leave my computer?

GateRook stores its configuration and executes gateway logic locally. It connects to the infrastructure you configure and returns allowed tool results to your chosen AI client. If that client uses a cloud model, those results may go to its provider. Optional tunnels, notifications, and exports also connect to services you enable.

Does the AI get my database password?

Connection credentials are resolved by GateRook for the connector. They are not handed to the AI as connection settings. Locally stored secrets are encrypted, and tool results are masked before being returned. Control which data your tools can expose as well as which actions they can run.

Do I have to move my infrastructure?

No. GateRook connects to existing databases, logs, and services that your computer can reach. Keep your environments where they are and choose which connections to expose through an endpoint.

Can I try it on this page?

Yes. The interactive example lets you switch environments, approve or deny a simulated change, and see a read-only policy block a write. It runs entirely in your browser without connecting to real infrastructure.
